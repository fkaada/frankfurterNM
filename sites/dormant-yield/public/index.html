    Who this is for

    Whoever now controls the assets

    A closed company cannot license anything itself. The person with authority over its assets has to.

    • Administrators and liquidatorsUK insolvency practitioners realising assets for creditors.
    • Bankruptcy trustees and ABC assigneesUS officeholders selling estate assets, usually under court supervision.
    • Buyers of the business assetsIf you acquired the records in a pre-pack or asset sale, check what the sale agreement covered.
    • Former directorsYou usually cannot apply yourself. Pass this page to the officeholder.
    Rights check

    Four checks before you talk to any buyer

    Most dormant data never gets licensed because nobody can show they are allowed to license it. Settle these first.

    Authority

    Who controls the company's assets today, and can they sign a licence? Get this in writing.

    Records quality

    Are the records complete, readable and still accessible? Lapsed software subscriptions often mean lost exports.

    Coverage

    Which years, systems and data types exist, and are they in English?

    Permissions

    Customer contracts, confidentiality clauses, software terms and employee data can each limit what may be shared.

    United Kingdom

    • In administration or liquidation, the officeholder controls the assets and decides.
    • Once a company is dissolved, its remaining property generally passes to the Crown as bona vacantia (Companies Act 2006, s.1012). Nobody can usually license it unless the company is first restored to the register.
    • Personal data stays covered by UK GDPR whoever holds it.

    United States

    • In bankruptcy, the trustee sells estate assets with court approval (11 U.S.C. §363).
    • If the company's privacy policy restricted transfers of personal information, the court may appoint a consumer privacy ombudsman before a sale (11 U.S.C. §§332, 363(b)(1)).
    • Outside bankruptcy, state law on dissolved corporations decides who can still act for the company.

    Other countries micro1 covers have their own insolvency and data protection rules. Take local advice. General information only, not legal advice.

    What is licensed

    Operational records, with people taken out

    Usually of interest

    • Support tickets and how they were resolved
    • Project and task histories
    • Internal documents, wikis and process guides
    • CRM and sales pipeline activity
    • Slack or Teams threads and email about the work

    Handle with care or leave out

    • Employee and HR records: the most sensitive part of any company's data
    • Customer personal data, unless properly de-identified
    • Payment card and bank account data
    • Health, children's or biometric data
    • Privileged or client-confidential material

    FAQ

    Questions people actually ask

    Do I pay anything?

    No. We charge nothing. Any referral fee is paid by micro1 and is disclosed on this page.

    Will you see our records?

    No. Records go only to the buyer, under its own agreement, after you have signed. Never send records to us or to anyone who asks for them before a contract.

    The company was struck off years ago. Can I still do this?

    In the UK, a dissolved company's property generally passes to the Crown until the company is restored to the register, so nobody can usually license its records directly. Restoration is possible in some cases; speak to a solicitor first. In the US it depends on state law and whether the company went through bankruptcy.

    What about employee data?

    Employee and HR records are the most sensitive part of company data. Expect them to be excluded or properly de-identified, and check whether consent or notice is needed before anything is shared.

    How long does it take?

    micro1 says it reviews and approves the data within 30 days, then pays within 30 days of invoice. Confirming who holds the rights can take longer than either.

    I was a director, not the officeholder. Can I apply?

    Usually not. While a company is in administration, liquidation or bankruptcy, the officeholder controls its assets. Pass this page to them.

    Which countries are covered?

    micro1's defunct-company track lists companies based in the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, India, Australia, Singapore, Ireland, the Netherlands, Germany and France. Our rights guidance covers the UK and the US in detail; elsewhere, take local advice.

    Which industries qualify?

    micro1 lists its defunct-company track as open to all industries. Records need to be in English, and the company needs 30 or more employees at its peak.

    Our company is still trading. Is this for us?

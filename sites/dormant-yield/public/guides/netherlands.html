      Netherlands: licensing a closed company's records

      A short guide for SMEs and enterprises. The steps are the same everywhere; the people and laws differ. Take local advice before you agree anything.

      Usually decidesThe curator (bankruptcy trustee)
      Insolvency lawBankruptcy Act (Faillissementswet)
      Personal dataGDPR
      Cost to youNothing from us
      Who usually decides in NetherlandsThe curator (bankruptcy trustee)
      On micro1's list
      1. 1

        Find who controls the assets

        In Netherlands, that is usually the curator (bankruptcy trustee) under the Bankruptcy Act (Faillissementswet). Confirm their authority in writing.

      2. 2

        Check the company's status

        Check the national company register for the current status and any appointed officeholder. A dissolved company may need restoring before anything can be licensed.

      3. 3

        Export the records

        List each system, the years it covers and roughly how much it holds. micro1 lists English as the language for this track.

      4. 4

        Take people out

        Personal data stays protected under the GDPR. Employee and HR records are usually left out entirely.

      5. 5

        Run the fit check, then apply

        micro1 lists Netherlands among ten countries for companies that are no longer operating, with 30+ peak employees company.micro1.ai/get-started · 15 Nov 2026.

      6. 6

        Take local advice and decide

        Our detailed rights guides cover the UK, US, Canada and Australia only. Ask a local insolvency or data protection adviser before you agree anything.

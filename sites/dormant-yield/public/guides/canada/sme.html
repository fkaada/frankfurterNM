      Guide for SMEs
      1. 1

        Confirm who can sign

        In bankruptcy, the licensed insolvency trustee holds and realises the company's property (Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act). Get the authority in writing before you speak to any buyer.

      2. 2

        Check the company's status

        The Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy lists insolvency records; Corporations Canada and provincial registries show status. When a federal corporation is dissolved, property it had not disposed of generally vests in the Crown (Canada Business Corporations Act, s.228). Provincial laws are similar.

      3. 3

        Export the records now

        List the tools the company used, then export from each one while access still works. Check the records are in English.

      4. 4

        Take people out

        PIPEDA, and provincial laws in Quebec, British Columbia and Alberta, continue to apply to personal information in the records. Employee and HR records are usually left out entirely.

        Run the fit check, then apply

      6. 6

        Review, take advice, sign or walk away

        micro1 says it reviews and approves the data within 30 days, then pays within 30 days of invoice. In CCAA proceedings and court-appointed receiverships, sales need court approval. Take advice on the contract; you can stop at any point before signing.

      Tips for SMEs

      • Act within weeks. Software subscriptions lapse after closure and exports disappear with them.
      • One person usually knew every system. Get their help with exports before they move on.
      • Keep it simple: one export per system, one adviser, one decision.
      • Ask the former IT provider or MSP for admin access and a list of tools in use.

      Common pitfalls

      • When a federal corporation is dissolved, property it had not disposed of generally vests in the Crown (Canada Business Corporations Act, s.228). Provincial laws are similar.
      • Sending a sample before a contract covers handling and confidentiality.
      • Leaving employee or customer personal data in the export.

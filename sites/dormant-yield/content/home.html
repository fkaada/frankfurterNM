Step 1 · Who this is for

Whoever now controls the assets

A closed company cannot sign anything itself. The person now legally in charge of its assets has to. In insolvency that person is called the officeholder. Guide for officeholders →

  • Administrators and liquidatorsUK licensed insolvency practitioners selling a company's assets to pay its creditors.
  • Bankruptcy trustees and assigneesUS trustees in Chapter 7 cases, and assignees in an assignment for the benefit of creditors, a state-law alternative to bankruptcy.
  • Licensed insolvency trustees and receiversCanadian professionals who sell a company's property for its creditors.
  • Liquidators and voluntary administratorsAustralian registered liquidators and administrators in control of company property.
  • Buyers of the business assetsIf you bought the business or its assets, for example in a pre-pack, check whether the sale covered the records.
  • Former directorsOnce a company is in insolvency, directors usually lose control of its assets. Pass this page to the officeholder.
Step 2 · What is licensed

How the work was done, with the people taken out

AI developers want to learn how real businesses solved problems: how a ticket got fixed, how a project ran. They do not want to know who anyone was. A licence gives permission to use the records on agreed terms; nothing is sold outright.

Step 5 · Before you talk to a buyer

Four checks before you talk to any buyer

Most records from closed companies are never licensed because nobody can show they are allowed to license them. Settle these first, or work through the readiness checklist.

Authority

Who controls the company's assets today, and can they sign a licence? Get this in writing.

Records quality

Are the records complete, readable and still accessible? Lapsed software subscriptions often mean lost exports.

Coverage

Which years, systems and data types exist, and are they in English?

Permissions

Customer contracts, confidentiality clauses, software terms and employee data can each limit what may be shared.

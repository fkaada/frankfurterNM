An assignment for the benefit of creditors (ABC) is a state-law alternative to bankruptcy. The company transfers its assets to an assignee, who realises them and distributes the proceeds to creditors. In some states the process is supervised by a court; in others it largely is not.

Are records an asset?

Often, yes. Operational records, such as support tickets, project histories, internal documents and team chat, can be licensable to AI developers once personal data is removed. For an assignee, that can be a possible extra asset for creditors that would otherwise be lost when software subscriptions lapse.

What to check first

  • Scope of the assignment. Does the assignment document cover intangible assets and data, not just equipment and receivables?
  • Privacy promises. What did the company's privacy policy and customer contracts say about transferring data? State privacy laws may also apply.
  • Employee data. Set HR and employee records aside. They are the most sensitive part of company data.

How it differs from bankruptcy

In a bankruptcy, sales outside the ordinary course need court approval under 11 U.S.C. §363, and where the privacy policy barred transfers of personal information, a consumer privacy ombudsman must be appointed before the court can approve selling it. ABCs follow state law instead, so take local advice. Our US rights guide covers both.

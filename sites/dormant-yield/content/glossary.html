Glossary

The words behind closed companies, in plain English

Insolvency and data law use their own vocabulary. Each term below gets one or two plain sentences and the countries where you will meet it. General information, not legal advice; the exact meaning depends on local law.

PeopleProcessesWhen a company has goneData and licensing

People

Officeholder
The person legally in charge of a closing or closed company's affairs, usually a licensed insolvency professional: an administrator, liquidator, trustee or receiver. They, not the former directors, decide what happens to the company's assets, including its records. All countries
Insolvency practitioner
A licensed professional who can act as administrator or liquidator of a company. UK, Ireland
Bankruptcy trustee
The person who takes control of the assets in a bankruptcy case and sells them for creditors. In the US, Chapter 7 trustees are usually appointed through the US Trustee Program. US
Licensed insolvency trustee
The professional licensed to run bankruptcies and proposals under Canada's Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act. Canada
Receiver
Someone appointed by a lender or a court to take control of particular assets, often so a secured lender can be repaid. UK, Canada, Australia, Ireland
Assignee
The person a company transfers all its assets to in an assignment for the benefit of creditors. The assignee sells them and pays creditors. US
Creditors
People and businesses the company owes money to: lenders, suppliers, staff, tax authorities. Money raised from the company's assets goes to them in a set order.

Processes

Administration
A formal process where an administrator takes control to rescue the company or get a better result for creditors than an immediate liquidation would. UK
Liquidation
The company's assets are sold, the money is shared among creditors, and the company is then dissolved. It can be voluntary or ordered by a court. All countries, under different names
Pre-pack
A sale of the business agreed before an administrator is appointed and completed straight after. Whether the records went to the buyer depends on the sale agreement. UK
Chapter 7
US bankruptcy liquidation: a trustee sells the company's assets and pays creditors. US
Chapter 11
US bankruptcy reorganisation: the business usually keeps running under court supervision while it restructures or sells itself. US
Section 363 sale
A sale of assets out of a US bankruptcy case, approved by the court under section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code. If the assets include personal data, the court can appoint a consumer privacy ombudsman to advise on it. US
Assignment for the benefit of creditors (ABC)
A state-law alternative to bankruptcy: the company hands its assets to an assignee, who sells them without a bankruptcy court. US
CCAA
Canada's Companies' Creditors Arrangement Act, used to restructure larger companies under court supervision. Canada
Voluntary administration
An administrator takes control to work out whether the company can be saved. Creditors then vote on a deal or on liquidation. Australia

When a company has gone

Dissolved or struck off
The company has been removed from the official register and no longer legally exists. It cannot sign anything, including a licence. All countries
Bona vacantia
Latin for "ownerless goods". When a UK company is dissolved, property it still owned usually passes to the Crown, or to the Duchy of Lancaster or Duchy of Cornwall. UK
Restoration
Putting a dissolved company back on the register so that its property can be dealt with again. Canada calls it revival. Rules and time limits depend on the country. UK, Ireland, Australia, Canada
Deregistration
Removal of an Australian company from ASIC's register. Property it still owned generally vests in ASIC. Australia

Data and licensing

Operational records
Everyday working material: support tickets, project histories, internal guides, sales pipeline activity and team chat. These show how work was done.
Personal data
Any information about an identifiable person, such as a name, email address or HR file. Data protection law (for example GDPR in the UK and EU, PIPEDA in Canada, the Privacy Act in Australia) still applies after a company closes.
De-identification
Removing or masking names, contact details and anything else that could identify a person, so what remains shows patterns of work, not people.
Data licence
Permission to use records for agreed purposes and on agreed terms. Unlike a sale, the terms can limit what the buyer may do with the data.
Peak employees
The highest number of people the company employed at any one time. micro1's defunct-company track asks for 30 or more.
Referral link
A link that tells micro1 an application came through us. If a qualifying deal completes, micro1 may pay us a referral fee. You pay nothing and your payment is not reduced.
